How the Command Center actually works

A field guide for reading the system: what the rules are, how to tell where a problem came from, and who can fix it. Written for the person who sees something wrong on screen and needs to know what to do next.

PART 1

The one thing to understand first

Everything else in this guide follows from this paragraph.

The Command Center is a face on Monday.com — not a database. Every fact about a patient lives in a column on a Monday board. The app reads those columns to build your queues, and writes to those columns when you act. It stores nothing of its own.

Patients don't move between stages because the app moves them. Each board has one switch — the Stage Advancer — and Monday's own automations watch it. The app's job is to gather the data for a stage, confirm it landed, and then flip that switch last. Monday takes it from there.

The fork every diagnosis starts at

Open the patient on the Monday board and look at the actual columns.

If the board is wrong, something wrote the wrong value — or nothing wrote at all. If the board is right but the screen disagrees, the data is fine and a rule in the app is filtering or counting it wrong. Those are two completely different problems with two different owners, and you can tell them apart in about thirty seconds.

PART 2

Five things can be at fault

You don't need to know how any of them work internally. You need to know which one you're looking at, because that decides who can fix it and how long it takes.

Board data

A value in a column is simply wrong or missing. Anyone with board access fixes it on the board. Minutes.

Board setup

A column, a status label, or an automation was renamed, deleted, or switched off. A board admin fixes it in Monday. No code.

A bot

A Railway service owns those columns — eligibility, DVS, doctor lookup. Backend work. The app can't fix it and isn't at fault.

The save

The app tried to write and it didn't land, or landed late. Check, then retry or escalate to Josh.

App rules

Which queue, whether it's due, which chart it appears on. Code change. Slowest — and see Part 5.

PART 3

The three rules that decide where a patient shows up

Nearly every "where did this patient go?" question is one of these three. They're applied in order, and a patient has to pass all three to appear in somebody's queue.

RULE 1Which queue — board, then group, then a splitter column

A patient's board and group get them most of the way. But several stages are split into more than one queue by a single status column, and that's where confusion lives:

  • Chase Clinicals splits on Clinicals Method: Fax or blank goes to Chase — Fax; Parachute or Email goes to Chase — Email & Parachute. Email rides with Parachute on purpose; it is not a bug.
  • Profile Send Off splits three ways, checked in this order: Already In System = Yes → Already In System; else Referral Type = Patient or Referral Source = CareCentrix → Patient Intake; else → Verified Referrals.
  • Medical Evaluation is one group split by the Stage Advancer into Evaluate → Send Request → Confirm Receipt → Chase → Doctor Appointments.

The classic trap: Referral Source also has a label called Patient, but only Referral Type routes a patient to Patient Intake. Two columns, same word, different meaning.

RULE 2Is the patient due today — and the rule differs by board

This is the one people get wrong most often, because there isn't a single rule. There are three:

StageCounts as due when…
Medical Evaluation
(Evaluate, Send Request, Confirm Receipt, Chase, Doctor Appts)		Next Action Date is blank, today, or in the past
Benefits, Submit AuthFollow Up status is not set to “Follow Up”
Auth Outstanding, DVSFollow Up Date is not in the future. The status column is ignored here, and a blank date counts as due.
Welcome Call, Profile Send OffFollow Up status is not set to “Done”
Scheduled CallsThe appointment time hasn’t passed yet today — the count falls through the day

So "why is this patient still showing?" and "why did they vanish?" can both be a date or a status, depending on which stage you're standing in.

RULE 3Escalation hides the patient from the rep — on purpose

An escalated patient leaves the rep's queue and the rep's count entirely and appears in a manager column in Pipeline Oversight instead. That's what escalating is for. There are two rungs:

  • Manager Escalation Required → the Manager Intervention column.
  • Final Escalation Required → the Final Decisions column. On the Medical Evaluation board this one means a rep has proposed the patient be marked stuck and is waiting on a manager's yes or no. It is a proposal, not a decision.

If a rep says a patient disappeared, check this column before anything else. It explains the large majority of them, and nothing is broken.

The failure this system is prone to

A patient can end up in no queue at all, and nothing anywhere will say so. If a patient reaches a state that no rule accounts for, they are simply not counted and not listed — no error, no warning, no red flag. They just quietly stop being worked.

This is why "nothing looks wrong" is not evidence that nothing is wrong, and why it's worth reporting a patient who feels stuck even when you can't point at anything specific.

PART 4

Triage — symptom to owner

Find the symptom, do the one check, read the chip. The chip tells you who can fix it.

Family A — “Where is my patient?”

A patient vanished from a rep's queue

Board data
Check first
The Escalation column on the item, then the follow-up date or status for that stage (Rule 2).
Usually
Working as designed. Escalated → they're in a manager column. Snoozed → they'll come back on the follow-up date.
What changes
Nothing, or a date fixed on the board.

A patient is in nobody's queue and nobody's count

App rules
Check first
The Stage Advancer value on the item. Is it a stage you recognize, or something unusual?
Usually
The patient reached a state no rule covers, so every filter skips them silently.
What changes
Code — a missing branch in the queue rules. Report it with the item link; this one never fixes itself.

The same patient shows up in two queues or two charts

App rules
Check first
Which two, exactly. Write both down — the overlap itself is the diagnosis.
Usually
Two filters that were meant to be mutually exclusive both match this patient.
What changes
Code. Harmless to work in the meantime, but it inflates counts.

A patient is on the right board but the wrong queue

Board data
Check first
The splitter column for that stage — Clinicals Method, or the three Profile Send Off columns (Rule 1).
Usually
The splitter holds a value nobody expected, or is blank when it shouldn't be.
What changes
Set the column correctly on the board and the patient moves queues on the next refresh.

Family B — “I saved it and it didn't stick”

A rep swears they saved it; the board is blank

The save
Check first
Open the item on Monday and read its Activity Log. Was there any write at that time?
Then
No write at all → it never left the browser; have them redo it and watch for an error. A write that was then overwritten → an automation or a bot changed it after; that's board setup or a bot.
What changes
Depends on which of the two — the Activity Log is what tells you.

It saved, but the patient never moved to the next stage

Board setup
Check first
Did the Stage Advancer actually flip on the item?
Then
It didn't flip → the app deliberately stopped, because it couldn't confirm the data saved first. That's the safety net working; the data would have gone downstream stale. It flipped but nothing moved → the Monday automation is off, deleted, or erroring.
What changes
Re-run the save, or a board admin checks the automation.

The app said “queued — don't repeat this”

The save
Check first
Nothing. Wait.
Meaning
The save is sitting in a durable queue on the server and will run. It is not lost.
What changes
Nothing — and importantly, do not redo the save. Repeating it writes twice.

One column reads blank for every patient, everywhere

Board setup
Check first
Does that column still exist on the board, and was it recently deleted and re-added?
Usually
Deleting and recreating a column gives it a new hidden ID. The app keeps asking for the old one and gets nothing back — with no error.
What changes
Code, to point at the new ID. Rename columns freely; never delete and recreate one.

A status label suddenly has a near-duplicate on the board

Board setup
Check first
The board's label list for that column — look for two entries differing only in capitalization or spacing.
Usually
A label was renamed. Some writes match labels by exact text, and Monday quietly creates a new label rather than failing.
What changes
A board admin merges the labels back. Flag any status-label rename before it happens.

Family C — “The numbers don't agree”

A role bar and an Oversight chart disagree

App rules
Check first
Whether the difference is a handful of patients or a large gap, and whether they're escalated.
Usually
The same rule is written in several places and one copy drifted. See Part 5 — this is the system's most common class of bug.
What changes
Code, in every place the rule lives.

The burndown shows a role as “not connected”

App rules
Check first
Whether it's a newly added role.
Usually
The start-of-day snapshot doesn't know about that role yet, so it shows "not connected" rather than a misleading zero. That's deliberate.
What changes
Code, in the two snapshot generators.

Start-of-day numbers look wrong all day

App rules
Check first
Whether live counts look right. If live is fine and only the comparison is off, it's the snapshot.
Usually
The snapshot is taken once each weekday morning. If its rules drift from the live ones, you get phantom in/out movement all day.
What changes
Code. It self-corrects the next morning if the underlying rule was already fixed.

Family D — “An outside system didn't do its job”

Eligibility / Stedi results are blank

A bot
Check first
Whether Name, DOB, insurance and Member ID were filled in before the check was run.
Usually
The app never writes eligibility results — a separate service does, one column at a time over 15–25 seconds. A permanently blank result means that service didn't write it.
What changes
Backend, not the app. Corrections are made through Profile Send Off, not on the Insurance board.

DVS or claims aren't progressing

A bot
Check first
The DVS and claims status columns on the item.
Usually
DVS is fully automatic — the page is a read-only monitor. A failed or manual-review result escalates the patient to a manager by board automation.
What changes
Backend, or a manager works the escalation.

A fax or email didn't send

A bot
Check first
Whether the person is still signed in, and whether the recipient address or fax number is right on the item.
Usually
Sending runs through a separate mail service and RingCentral. Failures there don't touch Monday at all.
What changes
Usually a re-send. Persistent failures are backend.

Incoming calls stopped popping up on screen

A bot
Check first
The person's own ring preference — all, list, or off. A narrowed list silences their screen only.
Then
If it's off for everyone, the call feed itself is down. There's a health check for exactly this; ask Josh to look.
What changes
A preference, or backend. Note this never makes a call unanswerable by someone else.

Blank white screen, or a page won't load

The save
Check first
Reload the tab once.
Usually
The tab was left open across a new release. The app is built to reload itself once when this happens, but a hard refresh always clears it.
What changes
Nothing. If a reload doesn't fix it, then report it.
PART 5

Why a fix sometimes only half-works

This explains more confusing behavior than anything else in the system, and it's worth knowing even if you never touch the code.

A rule like "Email patients belong with Parachute, not Fax" isn't written once. The same rule has to be repeated in five separate places, because five different parts of the app answer five different questions:

WhereThe question it answers
The role pageWhich patients appear in this rep's sidebar right now
The countsWhat number shows on the role bar
Oversight chartsWhich manager chart this patient lands on
The build-time snapshotStart-of-day baseline, early morning
The 9 AM snapshotStart-of-day baseline, the one used most days

If a change lands in four of the five, nothing errors. You get a queue that's right and a count that's wrong, or a chart that shows a patient the sidebar doesn't. That mismatch is a signature — when you see a number and a list disagree, this is almost always why.

What this means for you practically: when reporting an issue, say every place you saw it — "the bar says 12, the list shows 9, and the Oversight chart says 14" is far more useful than "the count is wrong."

PART 6

Where to actually look

Four places, in the order you should use them.

ToolAnswersWho can use it
The Monday item's Activity Log Who changed which column, to what, and exactly when. This is the single most useful tool in the whole system and it's built into Monday. Anyone with board access
Pipeline Oversight Where escalated patients went, and which manager column is holding them. If a patient left a rep's queue, they should be visible here. Managers
The gateway's audit log Every request the app made — so you can prove whether a save was even attempted. Metadata only; no patient data is stored there. Josh (it's key-protected)
The health checks Whether the plumbing is alive — the gateway, texting, and the incoming-call feed each have one. Josh

Reporting an issue so it can be fixed the same day

The fastest reports answer the fork in Part 1 before anyone has to ask. Send this:

Patient: [name + link to the Monday item] Stage/queue: [which page or chart you were on] Expected: [what you thought would happen] Saw: [what actually happened] When: [date + roughly what time] On the board: [what the relevant columns actually say right now] Also seen in: [any other count, list or chart that disagrees]

The last two lines are the ones that save the most time. "On the board" settles whether it's a data problem or an app problem, and "also seen in" points straight at the Part 5 mismatch.

TERMS

Words you'll hear

Roughly in the order they'll come up.

Stage Advancer
The one status column per board that Monday's automations watch. Flipping it is what moves a patient to the next stage. The app always writes it last.
Next Action Date (NAD)
When a patient should next surface in a queue. On the Medical Evaluation board, a blank or past date means due now.
Follow Up / Follow Up Date
The snooze mechanism on the other boards. Which of the two actually controls the queue depends on the stage — see Rule 2.
Escalation
Moves a patient out of a rep's queue and into a manager column. Two rungs: Manager Intervention, then Final Decisions.
Propose Stuck
A rep's request that a patient be pulled from the pipeline. It's a proposal only — a manager approves or returns it. It shows as “Final Escalation Required.”
Verified write
The safety net: the app saves the data, reads it back to confirm it landed, and only then flips the Stage Advancer. If it can't confirm, it stops rather than sending stale data downstream.
Gateway
The server the app talks to Monday through. Holds the credentials and logs every request, which is what makes an audit possible.
Baseline
The start-of-day count snapshot the burndown compares against, taken each weekday morning.
Stedi
The insurance eligibility check. A separate service runs it and writes the results back to Monday — the app only asks for it and waits.
DVS
The automatic Medicaid verification stage. Fully bot-run; the page is a read-only monitor.
Parachute
An outside source for clinical documents. In the app, Email and Parachute share one queue.
Masheke / Samantha
Long-standing internal nicknames for the Medical Evaluation board and the Insurance board. Same boards, older names.
